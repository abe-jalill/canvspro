# App Store Review for CoursePro's first iOS submission (2026): completeness, metadata, design, IP, extensions and common rejections

Scope and status: research done 2026-10-19. The live App Review Guidelines page says "Last Updated: October 16, 2026" ([Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). All guideline quotes below come from that live page unless a note says otherwise. Instructure's own domains (instructure.com, canvas.instructure.com and the school mirrors) could not be fetched from this environment. Instructure policy text therefore comes from search-result excerpts of those pages and is marked as such.

## 1. Guideline 2.1 App Completeness, and demo access for an app that needs a third-party (Canvas) account

### Takeaway
Guideline 2.1 requires a working demo account and a live backend. A built-in demo mode "in lieu of a demo account" now explicitly needs **prior approval by Apple**. For CoursePro, the safest approach is to give App Review a real, working Canvas connection (a test Canvas account plus a token, with step-by-step notes), keep sample-data mode as a backup, and explain both in the Review Notes. Relying only on unapproved sample data is a real 2.1 risk.

### Cited Findings
- 2.1(a), verbatim: "Submissions to App Review, including apps you make available for pre-order, should be final versions with all necessary metadata and fully functional URLs included; placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed before submission. Make sure your app has been tested on-device for bugs and stability before you submit it, and include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login. If you are unable to provide a demo account due to legal or security obligations, you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple. Ensure the demo mode exhibits your app's full features and functionality. We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems." — [App Review Guidelines 2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.1)
- 2.1(b): in-app purchases must be "complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional"; any IAP that can't be found must be explained in review notes. — [Guidelines 2.1(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.1)
- 2.3.1(a): "All new features, functionality, and product changes must be described with specificity in the Notes for Review section of App Store Connect (generic descriptions will be rejected) and accessible for review." — [Guidelines 2.3.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.1)
- The "Before You Submit" checklist says: "Provide App Review with full access to your app. If your app includes account-based features, provide either an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode, plus any other hardware or resources that might be needed to review your app (such as login credentials or a sample QR code)." Its other items are "Enable backend services so that they're live and accessible during review", "Include detailed explanations of non-obvious features ... in the App Review notes", "Test your app for crashes and bugs", "Ensure that all app information and metadata is complete and accurate" and "Update your contact information in case App Review needs to reach you." — [Guidelines, Before You Submit](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple's "Provide demo accounts" help, verbatim: "Enter a username and password that work at the time of submission. Credentials that expire or change before your review starts may result in the submission being rejected." It also says: "If your app requires an authentication code in addition to or instead of login credentials, include it in the Notes field in advance. Without it, a call may be required to continue the review. Calls are typically completed within three to five business days." — [App Store Connect Help: Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)
- The same help page on demonstration mode: "If your app handles sensitive user information, or operates in a highly regulated industry, you can implement a demonstration mode instead of providing a demo account. A demonstration mode should show your app's full features and functionality using demonstration data." — [Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)
- The same help page asks for "**External services and platforms:** List the external services, tools, or platforms the app uses to deliver its core functionality (for example, data providers, authentication services, payment processors, or AI services)." — [Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)
- The same help page covers apps that use another party's IP: attach "Trademark or rights documentation, such as a signed contract, or email correspondence showing you have permission." — [Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)
- Apple's "Incomplete information" common issue: "If some features require signing in, provide a valid demo account username and password. If there are special configurations to set, include the specifics. If features require an environment that is hard to replicate or require specific hardware, be prepared to provide a demo video or the hardware." — [Apple: App Review, Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- Forum reports: reviewers have rejected apps when they couldn't get past login, for example because of IP geo-blocking, and in one case Apple said a demo video could not substitute for access to continue the review. These are secondary forum threads (search excerpts) and not verified in full. — [Apple Dev Forums 768141](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/768141), [Apple Dev Forums 727374](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727374), [Apple Dev Forums 849775](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/849775)

### Inferences
- CoursePro's "login" is pasting a token for a third-party service, so reviewers have nothing to log in with. Apple's wording sees two acceptable paths: (a) an "active demo account", which here means a working Canvas instance plus a token or credentials, or (b) a "fully-featured demo mode". The guideline text says (b) needs "prior approval by Apple" when used *in lieu of* a demo account. The help page frames demo mode as suited to "sensitive user information" or "regulated" apps. Student education records are arguably sensitive (FERPA), which gives a reasonable justification, but it is not automatic.
- Recommended approach, in order of strength:
  1. Provide a real Canvas test environment: a Canvas account you control (for example a free/trial Canvas instance or a test student in a course you teach) with realistic courses, assignments, grades and announcements. Paste a **long-lived token valid through the review window** into the Review Notes, along with the Canvas base URL. Also list the steps (Settings > paste URL > paste token). Check the token isn't expiring and the account has no MFA prompt.
  2. Also make sample-data mode prominent, for example a "Try with sample data" button on the token screen. In the notes, say that it mirrors 100% of features, including widgets, Live Activities and Shortcuts.
  3. If you can't provide a real Canvas connection, ask App Review **before** submission whether the demo mode is acceptable. Apple's App Review appointment or Contact App Review are the channels. Alternatively, explain in the notes why a real account can't be provided (legal/security: student records, and school tokens are tied to real students).
- Turn the backend on and keep it up during review. If any CoursePro server (push or sync) is involved, make sure it accepts the reviewer's traffic from any region and over IPv6 (2.5.5).
- Notes should name the external services: the Canvas LMS REST API (Instructure) and any CoursePro backend or push service (for example Supabase or a Cloudflare Worker, per the companion web stack).

### Gaps
- I could not find an official Apple statement on whether sample/demo data is acceptable for **third-party-account** apps specifically, as opposed to "legal or security obligations". Developer experience suggests demo modes are routinely accepted when clearly explained, but I found no authoritative source.
- I did not verify whether Instructure still offers a free "Free-for-Teacher" Canvas account in 2026. A search result shows a "Canvas Lite Terms and Conditions" page at the former Free-for-Teacher policy URL ([Instructure](https://www.instructure.com/policies/canvas-free-for-teacher-acceptable-use-policy)), which suggests a rename to "Canvas Lite"; the page itself was unreadable.

## 2. Guideline 2.3 Accurate Metadata: screenshots, name/subtitle/keywords, audiences, other platforms, and the "CanvasPro" vs "CoursePro" display-name mismatch

### Takeaway
Screenshots must show the app in use, using fictional data. Metadata can't carry trademarks or other app names. The App Store name and the on-device name must "be similar". A home-screen name of "CanvasPro" under an App Store name of "CoursePro" is a concrete 2.3.8 rejection risk. It is also a 4.1(c)/5.2.1 trademark risk, because "Canvas" is Instructure's product name. Set CFBundleDisplayName to "CoursePro" before submitting.

### Cited Findings
- 2.3: "...make sure all your app metadata, including privacy information, your app description, screenshots, and previews accurately reflect the app's core experience..." — [Guidelines 2.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3)
- 2.3.3: "Screenshots should show the app in use, and not merely the title art, login page, or splash screen." — [Guidelines 2.3.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.3)
- 2.3.4: "previews may only use video screen captures of the app itself." — [Guidelines 2.3.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.4)
- 2.3.7, verbatim: "Choose a unique app name, assign keywords that accurately describe your app, and don't try to pack any of your metadata with trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information, or other irrelevant phrases just to game the system. App names must be limited to 30 characters. Metadata such as app names, subtitles, screenshots, and previews should not include prices, terms, or descriptions that are not specific to the metadata type. App subtitles ... must follow our standard metadata rules and should not include inappropriate content, reference other apps, or make unverifiable product claims. Apple may modify inappropriate keywords at any time..." — [Guidelines 2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.7)
- 2.3.8, verbatim: "Metadata should be appropriate for all audiences, so make sure your app and in-app purchase icons, screenshots, and previews adhere to a 4+ age rating even if your app is rated higher. ... Use of terms like 'For Kids' and 'For Children' in app metadata is reserved in the App Store for the Kids Category. Remember to ensure your metadata, including app name and icons (small, large, Apple Watch app, alternate icons, etc.), are similar to avoid creating confusion." — [Guidelines 2.3.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.8)
- A real 2.3.8 rejection message quoted by a developer: "We noticed that the app name to be displayed on the App Store and the app name displayed on the device do not sufficiently match." The fix was to make the names similar and keep the bundle ID unchanged. — [velog.io developer post](https://velog.io/@chosh91/App-store-심사-리젝트-Guideline-2.3.8-Performance-Accurate-Metadata); see also [Apple Dev Forums 762189 "Resolving Guideline 2.3.8"](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/762189)
- 2.3.9: "You are responsible for securing the rights to use all materials in your app icons, screenshots, and previews, and you should display fictional account information instead of data from a real person." — [Guidelines 2.3.9](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.9)
- 2.3.10: "...don't include names, icons, or imagery of other mobile platforms or alternative app marketplaces in your app or metadata, unless there is specific, approved interactive functionality. ... Don't include irrelevant information." — [Guidelines 2.3.10](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.10)
- 2.3.5 (choose the most appropriate category; Apple may change it) and 2.3.6 (answer age rating questions honestly). — [Guidelines 2.3.5/2.3.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.5)
- New age-rating system: "Provide responses to the updated age rating questions for each of your apps by January 31, 2026, to avoid an interruption when submitting..." — [Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)
- 2.3.12: the "What's New" text must describe significant changes (relevant to updates, not v1.0). — [Guidelines 2.3.12](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.12)
- Screenshot specs: "You must include at least one screenshot and can upload up to 10 screenshots per device size." iPhone requires "At least one screenshot for iPhone with Dynamic Island" (6.9"/6.5" classes). iPad: "(if your app supports iPadOS): At least one screenshot for iPad 13-inch display", with the 13" set "Required if app runs on iPad" (2064×2752 or 2048×2732 portrait). Formats are .jpeg/.jpg/.png with no alpha. — [App Store Connect Help: Screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications)
- Third-party guides agree that 1320×2868 (6.9") is the primary iPhone size and that Apple scales the 6.9" and 13" sets to smaller devices. — [Adalo 2026 guide](https://studio.adalo.com/blog/app-store-screenshot-sizes-2026)
- Apple's common-issues list includes "inaccurate screenshots (2.3.3)" and "misleading users (2.3)". — [Apple: Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)

### Inferences
- **Fix the display name.** "CanvasPro" on the home screen vs "CoursePro" on the store is the exact pattern of the quoted 2.3.8 rejection. The name also embeds Instructure's product name "Canvas", which 4.1(c) and 5.2.1 cover (see section 4). Set CFBundleDisplayName (and the widget, Live Activity and Shortcuts app-name strings) to "CoursePro". Keep the bundle identifier unchanged. Also check InfoPlist.strings localizations and the widget extension's display name.
- Don't use "Canvas", "Instructure", "Canvas Student" or competitor names in the **keyword field** or **subtitle**; 2.3.7 bars trademarks and other app names. A factual compatibility statement in the **description** ("Works with Canvas LMS", with a non-affiliation disclaimer) is the usual practice and is lower risk. It is still governed by 5.2.1 (see section 4).
- Screenshots: capture from sample-data mode, with fictional students, courses and grades (2.3.9). Show the dashboard, assignments, grades, widgets/Live Activity and the timer, not the token-paste screen (2.3.3). Don't show the Canvas logo or Instructure branding in screenshots.
- Don't mention Android, Google Play or "also on Android" (2.3.10). Mentioning the companion website is fine, but the metadata should focus on the iOS app.
- Category: Education (primary) and Productivity (secondary) fit. The age rating is probably 4+ under the new questionnaire. Avoid "For Kids" wording (reserved).
- If the app is iPhone-only but runs on iPad (the default for iPhone apps; 2.4.1 says iPhone apps "should run on iPad whenever possible"), iPad screenshots are not needed. If the target is Universal, a 13" iPad set is required, and the iPad layout will be reviewed.

### Gaps
- 2.3.7 states the 30-character limit only for the app name. Subtitle (30) and keyword field (100 characters) limits are widely known App Store Connect limits, but I did not fetch an Apple page confirming them in this session.
- I could not confirm a rumored September 2026 change to minimum screenshot coverage (one third-party blog mentions it; unverified). — [search summary of trysonar/adalo](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-store-screenshot-sizes.md)

## 3. Design: 4.0, 4.1 copycats, 4.2 minimum functionality / web wrappers, 4.3 spam, iPad, dark mode, Dynamic Type

### Takeaway
A native SwiftUI app with widgets, Live Activities, App Intents, offline-capable views and a timer clears 4.2 comfortably. The 4.2/4.2.2 risk applies to WebView wrappers and pure "content aggregators", so emphasise native features. Two traps: 4.3(b) now names "simple timers" as an over-saturated category, so don't market CoursePro as a Pomodoro app; and 4.1(c) bars using another developer's brand in your name or icon.

### Cited Findings
- 4.0: "...the following are minimum standards for approval to the App Store. ... Apps that stop working or offer a degraded experience may be removed from the App Store at any time." — [Guidelines 4.0](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#design)
- 4.1(a): "Don't simply copy the latest popular app on the App Store, or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." (b): "Submitting apps which impersonate other apps or services is considered a violation of the Developer Code of Conduct and may result in removal from the Apple Developer Program." (c): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." — [Guidelines 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#copycats)
- 4.2: "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store. If your App doesn't provide some sort of lasting entertainment value or adequate utility, it may not be accepted." — [Guidelines 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#minimum-functionality)
- 4.2.2: "Other than catalogs, apps shouldn't primarily be marketing materials, advertisements, web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links." — [Guidelines 4.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.2.2)
- 4.2.3(i): "Your app should work on its own without requiring installation of another app to function." — [Guidelines 4.2.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.2.3)
- 4.3(a): "Don't create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app ... If your app has different versions for specific locations, sports teams, universities, etc., consider submitting a single app..." — [Guidelines 4.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#spam)
- 4.3(b): "Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling, are well established on the App Store and we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience." — [Guidelines 4.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#spam)
- Trade coverage says the June 8–9, 2026 guideline revision added language against "low quality"/"mediocre" apps, plus changes touching AI, app quality, Live Activities, developer identity and App Store Connect information. These are secondary sources; I did not find Apple's own news post. — [conductatlas change log 2026-06-09](https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-06-09-apple-apple-app-store-review-guidelines-2759/), [appleworld.today June 2026](https://appleworld.today/2026/06/revamped-apple-guidelines-warn-developers-not-to-make-low-quality-and-mediocre-apps-for-the-app-store/)
- 2.4.1: "iPhone apps should run on iPad whenever possible." — [Guidelines 2.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.4.1)
- Apple's common-issues list includes "substandard UI", "web clippings (4.2)", "repeated similar apps (4.3)", "copycats (4.1)" and "not enough lasting value (4.2)". — [Apple: Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- 2.5.9: apps may not "block links out to other apps or other features that users would expect to work a certain way." — [Guidelines 2.5.9](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.9)

### Inferences
- CoursePro is a native client for a third-party service, not a wrapper of CoursePro's own website, so 4.2.2 "web clipping" doesn't fit. But it does aggregate Canvas content. Native-only value (widgets, Live Activities, Siri, a study timer tied to assignments, streaks, workload priority, offline cache, notifications) should be visible in the screenshots, the description and the Review Notes.
- Avoid in-app WebViews that just load Canvas pages as the main UI. Opening Canvas links in Safari/SFSafariViewController for details is fine.
- Pomodoro: present it as an integrated study session tied to assignments, with a Live Activity. Don't make "Pomodoro timer" the headline (4.3(b) "simple timers").
- Don't ship per-school variants (4.3(a)).
- iPad: an iPhone-only app still runs on iPad in compatibility mode and reviewers may test it there. Make sure the token-entry and settings screens don't break when the app runs on an iPad. If Universal, test split view and rotation, and supply 13" screenshots.
- Dark mode and Dynamic Type aren't explicitly required by a guideline I found. A layout that clips, overlaps or becomes unreadable with large text or in dark mode can draw a 4.0 ("substandard UI") or 2.1 ("obvious technical problems") rejection. Test both, and test widgets in dark, tinted and clear modes on iOS 26.

### Gaps
- I found no Apple text that mandates dark mode or Dynamic Type support; the conclusion above is an inference.
- I could not read Apple's own June 2026 change summary to confirm exactly what Live Activities language was added (domain fetch failed). The live guidelines' only Live Activities mention is 4.5.3 (see section 5).

## 4. Intellectual property (5.2.1, 5.2.2), the "Canvas" trademark, and Instructure's API policy on personal access tokens

### Takeaway
This is CoursePro's biggest non-technical risk. Canvas' own API documentation says that asking other users to manually generate a token and paste it into your app **violates Canvas' API Policy**, and that multi-user apps **must use OAuth**. Guideline 5.2.2 requires that you be "specifically permitted" by the service's terms, with "Authorization ... provided upon request". If App Review asks, a token-paste design is hard to defend. Plan for OAuth (a Canvas developer key from each school is the obstacle), or at least prepare a written justification. Separately, keep "Canvas" out of the app name and icon, and add a non-affiliation disclaimer.

### Cited Findings
- 5.2 intro: "Make sure your app only includes content that you created or that you have a license to use." — [Guidelines 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#intellectual-property)
- 5.2.1: "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission, and don't include misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata in your app bundle or developer name." — [Guidelines 5.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.1)
- 5.2.2: "If your app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request." — [Guidelines 5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.2)
- 4.1(c): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." — [Guidelines 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#copycats)
- Apple's common-issues page says documentation is required for "Third-party trademarks or content: guideline 4.1 and guideline 5.2". — [Apple: Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- Canvas OAuth documentation (official Instructure docs, mirrored on school Canvas instances; quoted from search excerpts because the pages were unreachable here): "For testing your application before you've implemented OAuth, the simplest option is to generate an access token on your user's profile page." It continues: "asking any other user to manually generate a token and enter it into your application is a violation of Canvas' API Policy." And: "Applications in use by multiple users MUST use OAuth to obtain tokens." And: "access tokens are password equivalent, so keep it secret." — [Canvas API docs: OAuth2 (UBC mirror)](https://canvas.ubc.ca/doc/api/file.oauth.html), [Colorado mirror](https://canvas.colorado.edu/doc/api/file.oauth.html)
- The same docs note they are moving to the "Instructure Developer Documentation Portal", with redirects after July 1, 2026. — [Canvas API docs mirror](https://canvas.ubc.ca/doc/api/file.oauth.html)
- Instructure Canvas API Policy (search-result excerpts; page not fetched): "Your application should not mirror or replicate Instructure, our products, or any other organization using our products. End users should understand that your application is integrated with Canvas but is an independent resource." Its prohibited conduct includes "Use business names and/or logos in a manner that can mislead, confuse, or deceive Customers or Users", and developers "should not remove or alter any proprietary notices in the Canvas API." — [Instructure Canvas API Policy](https://www.instructure.com/policies/canvas-api-policy)
- Instructure's Master Terms: "Use and access to the Application Program Interface ('API') will be subject to the Instructure API Policy." — [Instructure Master Terms](https://www.instructure.com/policies/master-terms-conditions)
- Developer keys (OAuth2 client ID/secret) are created and scoped by each institution's Canvas admin. Requests to ungranted scopes return 401. — [Canvas developer keys doc (Columbia mirror)](https://courseworks2.columbia.edu/doc/api/file.developer_keys.html)
- Some universities restrict or disable student personal access tokens and cite Instructure's API policy. Their concerns include MFA/SSO bypass and AI agents overloading Canvas, and they say the token holder is responsible for actions taken with the token. — [Univ. of Missouri System KB](https://tdx.umsystem.edu/TDClient/66/MOOnline/KB/ArticleDet?ID=2193), [UNH/PSU KB](https://td.usnh.edu/TDClient/60/Portal/KB/Article/5520/Canvas-User-API-Token-Access-UNH-PSU)
- Existing third-party Canvas clients: "HLS Grades" is a third-party app for self-hosted Canvas grades. Its developer notes the official Canvas apps are unavailable for self-hosted instances. I could not determine how it authenticates. — [GitHub tneotia/HLSGrades](https://github.com/tneotia/HLSGrades)
- The official Canvas Student app (Instructure, Inc.) already offers an iOS grades widget for up to eight courses. — [Canvas Community guide](https://community.canvaslms.com/docs/DOC-13991-18561817643), [UNCG listing](https://software.uncg.edu/Available/Canvas-Student-Mobile-App)

### Inferences
- **Name and icon:** "CoursePro" is fine. "CanvasPro" (store name, display name, icon text) would breach 4.1(c) without Instructure's approval, and is the main reason the rename matters. Don't use the Canvas logo, Instructure colors or trade dress in the icon or screenshots.
- **Descriptive use:** Saying "Works with Canvas LMS" in the description is nominative/descriptive use and common practice. Pair it with a clear disclaimer, for example: "CoursePro is an independent app and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Instructure, Inc. Canvas is a trademark of Instructure, Inc." Put it in the description and in-app (About/Settings, and the onboarding token screen). This also satisfies the API Policy's "independent resource" expectation. Keep "Canvas" out of keywords and the subtitle (2.3.7).
- **Token-paste model vs 5.2.2:** If App Review asks for "authorization", the developer has no Instructure permission, and Canvas' own docs call the token-paste model a policy violation for multi-user apps. Mitigations, from strongest to weakest:
  1. Implement OAuth2 (ASWebAuthenticationSession) where schools have issued a developer key, and keep token entry as an "advanced/self-hosted" option.
  2. Seek written permission or partner status from Instructure, and attach it in App Review Information (Apple accepts "email correspondence showing you have permission").
  3. At minimum, avoid describing token paste as the primary path in metadata. Explain in the notes that the user authorises access to their own data, that the token stays on device in the Keychain and is never sent to CoursePro servers (if true), and that the user can revoke it in Canvas.
- Whether App Review actually checks Canvas terms is uncertain. Reviewers often don't probe 5.2.2 unless a rights holder complains or the app's branding invites scrutiny. An Instructure complaint after launch could still cause removal. The rebrand away from "CanvasPro" lowers that visibility.
- Privacy: tokens are "password equivalent". Store them in the Keychain, never log them, and disclose the handling in the privacy policy.

### Gaps
- Direct fetches of instructure.com, canvas.instructure.com and the mirrored doc pages failed (DNS/proxy). The API Policy and OAuth quotes come from search-engine excerpts, not full-page reads. Re-verify the wording, and check whether the new Instructure Developer Documentation Portal changed it.
- I found no published Instructure trademark-usage guideline for third-party apps, and no App Store listing text from a third-party Canvas companion showing a disclaimer. I could not confirm how competing third-party Canvas iOS apps authenticate or describe themselves.
- I found no public report of an App Store rejection of a Canvas companion app under 5.2.2.

## 5. Extensions: widgets, Live Activities, App Intents / Siri / App Shortcuts (2.5.11, 2.5.16, 2.5.18, 4.4, 4.5.3)

### Takeaway
Extensions must relate to the app's content, carry no ads, marketing or IAP, and be disclosed in the marketing text. Live Activities must track a defined task with a start and end (CoursePro's study session or class countdown fits). They may run up to 8 hours, with at most 12 hours on the Lock Screen, and must end promptly. Siri/App Shortcut phrases must include the app name and must **not** include third-party service names such as "Canvas".

### Cited Findings
- 2.5.16: "Widgets, extensions, and notifications should be related to the content and functionality of your app." — [Guidelines 2.5.16](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.16)
- 2.5.18: "Display advertising should be limited to your main app binary, and should not be included in extensions, App Clips, widgets, notifications, keyboards, watchOS apps, etc." Ads may not target based on "school and classroom data (such as from ClassKit)". — [Guidelines 2.5.18](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.18)
- 4.4: "Apps hosting or containing extensions must comply with the App Extension Programming Guide ... and should include some functionality, such as help screens and settings interfaces where possible. You should clearly and accurately disclose what extensions are made available in the app's marketing text, and the extensions may not include marketing, advertising, or in-app purchases." — [Guidelines 4.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#extensions)
- 4.5.3: "Do not use Apple Services to spam, phish, or send unsolicited messages to customers, including Game Center, Push Notifications, Live Activities, etc." — [Guidelines 4.5.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.5.3)
- 4.5.4: "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in..." — [Guidelines 4.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.5.4)
- 2.5.11(i): "Apps integrating SiriKit and Shortcuts should only sign up for intents they can handle without the support of an additional app and that users would expect from the stated functionality." (ii): "Ensure that the vocabulary and phrases in your plist pertains to your app ... Aliases must relate directly to your app or company name and should not be generic terms or include third-party app names or services." (iii): "Resolve the Siri request or Shortcut in the most direct way possible and do not insert ads or other marketing between the request and its fulfillment." — [Guidelines 2.5.11](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.11)
- App Shortcuts: an Apple DTS engineer states the `\(.applicationName)` token is required in App Shortcut phrases, and omitting it produces build errors (including with Xcode 26/iOS 26). Developers report Siri recognition only after including the app name. — [Apple Dev Forums 799864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/799864), [Apple Dev Forums 712095](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/712095)
- HIG, Live Activities: "Offer Live Activities for tasks and events that have a defined beginning and end. Live Activities work best for tracking short to medium duration activities that don't exceed eight hours." "Don't use a Live Activity to display ads or promotions." "Avoid displaying sensitive information. Live Activities are prominently visible and could be viewed by casual observers ... display an innocuous summary..." "Make sure tapping the Live Activity opens your app at the right location." "Alert people only for essential updates..." "Always end a Live Activity immediately when the task or event ends, and consider setting a custom dismissal time." — [HIG: Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities)
- ActivityKit: "A Live Activity can be active for up to eight hours unless its app or a person ends it before this limit. ... a Live Activity remains on the Lock Screen for a maximum of 12 hours." "Static and dynamic data for a Live Activity, including data for ActivityKit updates and ActivityKit push notifications, can't exceed a combined size of 4 KB." Ended activities are removed after the dismissal date or four hours, "whichever comes first." — [ActivityKit: Displaying live data with Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)
- HIG, widgets: "Choose simple ideas that relate to your app's main purpose. Include timely content and relevant functionality." "Let people know when authentication adds value ... include a message like 'Sign in to view reservations' when people are signed out." Widgets "don't support continuous, real-time updates, and the system may adjust the limits for updates". "Ensure that a widget interaction opens your app at the right location." "Design placeholder content that helps people recognize your widget." — [HIG: Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)

### Inferences
- Widgets: when no token or data is present, show a clear "Open CoursePro to connect your courses" state, not an empty or broken widget. In sample-data mode, widgets should show sample data so the reviewer can see them. Deep-link taps to the relevant assignment or course.
- Live Activities: use them for the Pomodoro/study session and class countdowns (defined start and end, under 8 hours). End them when the session ends. Don't use them for a permanent "next assignment" ticker. On the Lock Screen, show innocuous text (for example "Study session – 18:42 left") rather than grades. Add `NSSupportsLiveActivities` to Info.plist.
- App Shortcuts: phrases like "Show my assignments in \(.applicationName)" are fine. Avoid phrases or aliases containing "Canvas" (2.5.11(ii) bars third-party service names). Because `.applicationName` resolves from the display name, the "CanvasPro" display name would put "Canvas" into every Siri phrase. That is another reason to rename to CoursePro.
- 4.4 disclosure: name the widgets, Live Activities and Siri Shortcuts in the description.
- Notifications: don't put grades in push text if they could count as "sensitive personal or confidential information" (4.5.4). Consider a "hide grade values in notifications" option.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact Live Activities wording that trade press says was added in the June 2026 guideline revision. The only mention on the live guidelines page is 4.5.3.
- I did not find an Apple page on ActivityKit push update budgets/frequent updates (`NSSupportsLiveActivitiesFrequentUpdates`) in this session.

## 6. Software requirements (2.5.x), SDK minimums, developer info (1.5), privacy policy (5.1.1), login services (4.8)

### Takeaway
Since April 28, 2026, every upload must be built with **Xcode 26+ and the iOS 26 SDK**. A deployment target of iOS 17 is still allowed; the minimum target is iOS 13 since September 9, 2026. The app must use public APIs only, run over IPv6-only networks and justify every background mode. It needs a working Support URL with contact info (1.5 calls this out for classroom apps) and a privacy policy linked both in App Store Connect **and** in the app.

### Cited Findings
- SDK minimum: "Since April 28, 2026 ... Apps uploaded to App Store Connect must be built with Xcode 26 or later using an SDK for iOS 26, iPadOS 26, tvOS 26, visionOS 26, or watchOS 26." — [Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)
- Minimum OS target: "Since September 9, 2026 ... iOS and iPadOS apps uploaded to App Store Connect must target iOS 13 or later." — [Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)
- Privacy manifests: "You'll need to include approved reasons for the listed APIs used by your app's code (including from third-party SDKs) to upload a new or updated app" (since May 1, 2024). — [Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)
- Toolchain (third-party): Xcode 26.4+ requires macOS Tahoe 26.2+. Building with the iOS 26 SDK opts the UI into Liquid Glass, and `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` is a temporary opt-out. — [Capgo blog](https://capgo.app/blog/xcode-26-requirement-for-capacitor-apps/), [stora.sh](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-04-01-ios-26-sdk-deadline-liquid-glass-ready)
- 2.5.1: "Apps may only use public APIs and must run on the currently shipping OS. ... Apps should use APIs and frameworks for their intended purposes and indicate that integration in their app description." — [Guidelines 2.5.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.1)
- 2.5.2: apps may not "download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app". — [Guidelines 2.5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.2)
- 2.5.4: "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local notifications, etc." — [Guidelines 2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.4)
- 2.5.5: "Apps must be fully functional on IPv6-only networks." — [Guidelines 2.5.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.5)
- 2.5.14: explicit consent and indication when recording user activity. — [Guidelines 2.5.14](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.14)
- 1.5: "Make sure your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you; this is particularly important for apps that may be used in the classroom. Failure to include accurate and up-to-date contact information not only frustrates customers, but may violate the law in some countries or regions." — [Guidelines 1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.5)
- Broken links: "All links in your app must be functional. A link to user support with up-to-date contact information and a link to your privacy policy is required for all apps." — [Apple: Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must identify the data collected and all uses, confirm that third parties provide equal protection, and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." — [Guidelines 5.1.1(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)
- 5.1.1(v): "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." — [Guidelines 5.1.1(v)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)
- 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." — [Guidelines 5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.2)
- 4.8 Login Services: a third-party/social login requires an equivalent privacy-preserving option (for example Sign in with Apple). It is not required if "Your app is an education, enterprise, or business app that requires the user to sign in with an existing education or enterprise account" or "Your app is a client for a specific third-party service and users are required to sign in to their mail, social media, or other third-party account directly to access their content." — [Guidelines 4.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.8)
- 3.1.1: unlocking features requires in-app purchase, not license keys or codes. — [Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.1)

### Inferences
- Background modes: declare only what's used. "Remote notifications" for push is fine. Don't declare `audio` to keep the Pomodoro running in the background; that's a classic 2.5.4 rejection. Use a Live Activity, `Text(timerInterval:)` and scheduled local notifications instead. Background App Refresh (BGTaskScheduler) for syncing assignments is fine.
- Canvas-only sign-in (a token or Canvas OAuth) falls under the 4.8 exemptions (education account / third-party-service client), so Sign in with Apple isn't required for that. If the iOS app also creates a **CoursePro account** (for example Google sign-in for sync with the web app), Sign in with Apple (or an equivalent) is required, and in-app account deletion is mandatory.
- The privacy policy must cover Canvas data (courses, grades), the token (stored on device or server?), push tokens, analytics and any AI features. It must also fill App Privacy "nutrition labels" consistently. Link it in the in-app Settings.
- Support URL: a real page with an email or contact form. Don't point it at a 404 or the marketing homepage without contact info.
- IPv6: Canvas hosts and any CoursePro backend must be reachable by hostname. Never hard-code IPv4 literals.
- Test the Liquid Glass appearance from building with the iOS 26 SDK, especially custom tab bars and the sidebar, since a broken layout is a 2.1/4.0 risk.

### Gaps
- No Apple announcement of an Xcode 27/iOS 27 SDK upload requirement was on the Upcoming Requirements page as of 2026-10-19. Historically Apple raises it each spring, so expect one around April 2027 (unconfirmed).

## 7. Most common first-submission rejections and practical process tips (notes, TestFlight, phased release, expedited review, App Review messages, appeals) — with checklist

### Takeaway
Apple says over 40% of unresolved issues fall under 2.1 App Completeness (crashes, placeholders, incomplete review information). Performance-section guidelines (2.x) appear on most rejections. For CoursePro, the likely first-try killers are, in order: reviewer can't get in or sees empty data (2.1), the display-name/trademark mismatch (2.3.8/4.1/5.2.1), missing or broken privacy/support links (5.1.1/1.5), a crash or layout bug on iPad or iOS 26, and a possible 5.2.2 query about Canvas terms.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness, which covers crashes, placeholder content, incomplete information, and more." Common issues listed: crashes and bugs, broken links, placeholder content, incomplete information, privacy policy issues, unclear data access requests (purpose strings), inaccurate screenshots, substandard UI, web clippings, repeated similar apps, copycats, misleading users, not enough lasting value, and submission by the incorrect entity. — [Apple: App Review – Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- Review times: "App Review works around the clock, typically reviewing at least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours. ... If your submission is incomplete, review times may be delayed or your submission may not pass." — [Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- App Store Transparency Report 2025 (figures via a search-tool summary of Apple's page, which could not be fetched directly): 9,100,620 submissions reviewed; 2,093,244 rejected; 387,087 approved after rejection. Rejections by section: Performance 1,354,418; Legal 495,673; Design 415,532; Business 283,820; Safety 151,159. Sections overlap because one submission can cite several. — [Apple 2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://images.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/). A third-party analysis puts the overall rejection rate at 24.85% (2024) and 23.00% (2025) — [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/tutorials/app-store-rejection-reasons) (secondary; verify).
- Purpose strings: "All apps submitted to App Store Connect that access user data are required to include a purpose string." — [Apple: Avoiding common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- After You Submit: rejections are handled by "us[ing] App Store Connect to communicate directly with the App Review team" (the old "Resolution Center" is now the App Review section of the app's page). Appeals go to the App Review Board. "Expedite Requests: If you have a critical timing issue, you can request an expedited review ... If we find you're abusing this system, we may reject your requests going forward." "If your app is repeatedly rejected for the same guideline violation ... review of your app will take longer." "It can take up to 24-hours for your app to appear on all selected storefronts." Bug Fix Submissions apply only to apps already on the store. — [Guidelines, After You Submit](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Expedite criteria: "fixing a critical bug in your app or releasing your app to coincide with an event you're directly associated with". Appeals: "Provide specific reasons why you believe your app complies ... Submit only one appeal per submission that didn't pass review. Respond to any requests for additional information before submitting an appeal." — [Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- App Review appointments: "Meet with App Review over Webex ... In each 30-minute video appointment, you can ask for advice on what to expect during review, how your app can best align with guidelines..." — [Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- An Information Needed call for missing auth codes takes "three to five business days". — [Complete review help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)

### Inferences
- TestFlight external testing goes through a lighter Beta App Review. It's a useful dry run of the demo flow, crashes and metadata, but approval there doesn't guarantee App Store approval. (General knowledge; no Apple page fetched on TestFlight review this session — see Gaps.)
- Use "Manually release this version" for v1.0, so you control launch timing after approval. Phased release mainly matters for updates with automatic updates.
- If rejected, reply in App Store Connect with specifics (and a screen recording if helpful) before resubmitting or appealing. Fix all cited items in one resubmission to avoid "repeatedly rejected" slowdowns.

#### First-try checklist for CoursePro (derived from the cited rules above)
1. **Build:** Xcode 26+ / iOS 26 SDK; deployment target iOS 17 is OK. Archive in Release mode, test on a physical device on iOS 26 (and iOS 17), and on an iPad in compatibility mode (or fully, if Universal). No crashes on the cold launch → paste token → sample data → widgets → Live Activity → Shortcut paths (2.1).
2. **Name consistency:** App Store name "CoursePro" (≤30 chars), CFBundleDisplayName "CoursePro", widget and extension display names, App Shortcut `.applicationName` phrases, icon without "Canvas" text or logo. Keep the bundle ID unchanged (2.3.8, 4.1(c), 5.2.1).
3. **Metadata:** Education category. No "Canvas"/"Instructure"/competitor names in the keywords or subtitle. Description may say "works with Canvas LMS" plus the non-affiliation disclaimer, and should list the widgets, Live Activities and Siri Shortcuts (4.4, 2.5.1). No Android mentions (2.3.10). No unverifiable claims ("#1", "official") (2.3.7).
4. **Screenshots:** From sample-data mode, with fictional names and grades (2.3.9). Show the dashboard, assignments, grades, widgets, Live Activity and timer, not the token screen (2.3.3). iPhone 6.9" (1320×2868) set; 13" iPad set only if the app supports iPadOS. Up to 10 per size, no alpha.
5. **Review access:** In App Review Information, give a working Canvas base URL plus a long-lived token or test credentials for a populated test course (no MFA). Give exact steps, and explain how to open sample-data mode as a fallback. Add a feature tour for non-obvious features (how to add a widget, start a study session to see the Live Activity, invoke the Siri phrase). List external services (Canvas REST API, CoursePro push backend). Make sure any backend is up for the full review window (2.1, 2.3.1).
6. **Demo-mode policy:** If sample data is the *only* reviewer path, request Apple's prior approval (App Review appointment or contact) or justify it in the notes (2.1(a)).
7. **IP and permissions (5.2.2):** Prepare a short statement on how CoursePro accesses Canvas (user-authorized access to the user's own data; token stored in the Keychain on device; revocable). Ideally move toward OAuth or get Instructure permission, because Canvas docs say asking users to paste tokens violates its API policy. Add the in-app disclaimer.
8. **Privacy:** Privacy policy URL in App Store Connect and in-app. It covers Canvas data, token handling, push tokens, retention/deletion and revoking access (disconnect plus delete local data). App Privacy labels match. Privacy manifest includes required-reason APIs (for example UserDefaults). Purpose strings exist for every permission requested, with notifications asked in context (5.1.1).
9. **Accounts:** If a CoursePro account exists, add in-app account deletion, and add Sign in with Apple if Google or other social login is offered (5.1.1(v), 4.8). Canvas-only sign-in is exempt under 4.8.
10. **Support:** A Support URL with a working contact method (1.5). All in-app links work (2.1, common issues).
11. **Extensions:** Widgets show useful content or a clear "connect" state, never blank, with deep links. Live Activities only for timed sessions or countdowns (≤8h), ended promptly, with no sensitive grades on the Lock Screen and no ads. Siri phrases include the app name and not "Canvas" (2.5.11, 2.5.16, 4.4, HIG).
12. **Background modes:** Only those used (remote notifications, background fetch/processing). No audio or location hacks to keep the timer alive (2.5.4). Works on IPv6-only networks (2.5.5).
13. **Pomodoro positioning:** Present it as part of an assignment-aware study workflow, not a standalone "simple timer" (4.3(b)).
14. **Age rating:** Answer the 2026 age-rating questionnaire (Jan 31, 2026 requirement). Metadata appropriate for 4+ (2.3.6/2.3.8).
15. **Submission hygiene:** No placeholder text, "coming soon" or debug menus. Accurate contact info in App Review Information. Choose manual release. Use TestFlight external beta as a rehearsal.
