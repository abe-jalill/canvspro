# Clearing CoursePro's App Review on the first try

CoursePro can realistically pass on its first submission, but only if the developer does a handful of things that the code cannot do for them. The code already meets most of Apple's rules: Sign in with Apple, in-app account deletion with Apple token revocation, privacy manifests, the AI-sharing disclosure, and a "CoursePro" name that matches across the store, the home screen and the widgets. What can still sink the review is mostly configuration and decisions. First, Sign in with Apple only works once the Apple Developer portal capability, the Supabase Apple provider and the server's Apple key secrets are all set. If they are missing, the reviewer's first tap on the Apple button or on "Delete account" fails, which is a 2.1 completeness rejection. Second, a server-side paywall that is switched off today would, if turned on later without in-app purchase, break 3.1.1 and the newly tightened 2.5.19 rule against changing an app after approval. Third, the App Store Connect fields are not filled in yet: an 18+ age-rating override, a privacy label that matches the manifest, working privacy and support links, and precise Notes for App Review that explain the sample-data review account. Under the live guidelines (last revised **October 16, 2026**), Sign in with Apple is not strictly required for an app that offers only its own email/password accounts. Now that CoursePro offers it, though, it has to follow Apple's button, data-handling and revocation rules exactly. Two lower-probability risks need a plan but do not block submission. One is Canvas's own API policy, which forbids asking users to paste personal access tokens (a 5.2.2 exposure). The other is lock-screen grade alerts (4.5.4). The checklist at the end separates what is blocking from what is recommended.

## Apple rejects most first submissions for completeness, not policy

Apple says that **over 40% of unresolved review issues fall under guideline 2.1, App Completeness**, which covers crashes, placeholder content and incomplete review information ([Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). That shapes the whole plan. CoursePro's highest risks are a reviewer who cannot get in, a reviewer who sees an empty app, or a reviewer who taps a button wired to a backend that has not been configured yet. Guideline 2.1(a) requires submissions to be "final versions with all necessary metadata and fully functional URLs." It also requires the developer to "include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login." It adds that a built-in demo mode used "in lieu of a demo account" needs "**prior approval by Apple**" ([App Review Guidelines 2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.1)). Apple's help page warns that "Credentials that expire or change before your review starts may result in the submission being rejected." It also says a missing authentication code can trigger a call that takes "three to five business days" ([App Store Connect Help: Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)). Review itself is fast, with **at least 50% of submissions reviewed in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48** ([Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). That makes the preparation, not the wait, the deciding factor.

**Status in CoursePro.** The review account `app.preview@canvaspro.app` signs in for real and shows sample Canvas data, the same data as the website demo. This is a genuine demo account, not an in-app demo mode, so the "prior approval" clause does not literally apply. The Canvas side, however, is simulated, and the reviewer never exercises the real connect-a-Canvas-account flow. The safest position is to say this plainly in the review notes: the account is real, its Canvas data comes from the backend, and it is not a hidden demo switch in the binary. Where possible, also give a working Canvas URL and a long-lived token for a test course you control, so the reviewer can test a live connection. Apple's help page asks submissions to list "External services and platforms" the app uses, "for example, data providers, authentication services, payment processors, or AI services" ([Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)). Name Supabase, the canvaspro.app backend, the Canvas LMS API, APNs and Sign in with Apple.

Two app-specific traps follow from the new Sign in with Apple support. A reviewer who signs in with their own Apple Account gets a brand-new, empty CoursePro account with no Canvas data. The empty state must explain how to connect Canvas, and the notes must point the reviewer to the review account for populated screens. A reviewer testing account deletion may also delete `app.preview@canvaspro.app` itself. Ask them in the notes to test deletion on an account they create, and be ready to recreate the review account if they don't.

| Requirement | Already done in code | You must do |
|---|---|---|
| Working login for reviewer (2.1) | Review account signs in and shows sample data | Confirm password, no MFA, valid through review; enter it in App Review Information |
| Live backend (2.1) | Backend exists | Keep canvaspro.app and Supabase up for the whole review window; IPv6 reachable (2.5.5) |
| Real third-party flow | Token-connect screen exists | Optional but recommended: provide a test Canvas URL plus a long-lived token |
| Demo-mode approval (2.1(a)) | Not an in-app demo mode | Explain the sample-data account in notes; optionally pre-clear it in an App Review appointment |

## Sign in with Apple is optional under 4.8 but now has to work perfectly

The current guideline 4.8 requires an "equivalent" privacy-preserving login only when an app "use[s] a third-party or social login service (such as Facebook Login, Google Sign-In …) to set up or authenticate the user's primary account." It exempts apps that "exclusively use[] your company's own account setup and sign-in systems" ([App Review Guidelines 4.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple dropped the by-name Sign in with Apple mandate in January 2024 ([Apple Developer News, Jan 25 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=7j1f99yf)). CoursePro offers no Google or other social login, so 4.8 never forced Sign in with Apple; the email/password system alone is exempt. The education exemption would not have helped either, because it covers apps that require "an existing education or enterprise account." CoursePro creates its own account and attaches Canvas afterwards. Adding Sign in with Apple is still a sound product choice. Once it ships, though, it is reviewed against Apple's design and data rules, and a broken Apple button is a 2.1 bug whatever 4.8 says.

**Button rules.** The Human Interface Guidelines require the button to be "no smaller than other sign-in buttons" and visible without scrolling. Titles are limited to "Sign in with Apple, Sign up with Apple, or Continue with Apple." Styles are black, white or white-outline, and the black style must not be used "on black or dark backgrounds." The minimum size is 140×30 pt ([HIG: Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple)). CoursePro uses the system `SignInWithAppleButton` at the same size as the main button, on both sign-in and create-account, in black or white. The one thing to verify by eye is that dark mode really switches to the white style.

**Data rules.** Reviewers reject apps that ask for a name or email after Apple sign-in, under 4.0 Design. The wording is "Your app requires users to provide their name and/or email address after using Sign in with Apple. This information is already provided by the Authentication Services framework" ([Apple Developer Forums 730223](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/730223)). Apple delivers the name only on the first authorization and tells developers to "immediately store it" ([Authenticating users with Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/authenticating-users-with-sign-in-with-apple)). Supabase says the name "must be captured from Apple's native authentication response and manually saved using the `updateUser` method" ([Supabase docs: Login with Apple](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/docs/content/guides/auth/social-login/auth-apple.mdx)). CoursePro saves the name once and never asks for name or email again, which is correct.

The new-account step that asks Apple users to confirm they are 18+ and accept the Terms and Privacy Policy is defensible. The HIG allows extra steps when the information is "required (legal or contractual)." Its warning is aimed at delays for "information that isn't required" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple)). Keep that screen to the legally required items only, and label it as required by the Terms. If the user declines, CoursePro deletes the account and revokes the Apple token, which is the right outcome.

**Nonce and token plumbing.** Supabase's server code hashes the nonce it receives and compares it with the identity token. If the two don't match, it fails with "Nonces mismatch." It also rejects tokens whose audience is not a configured Client ID ([supabase/auth token_oidc.go](https://github.com/supabase/auth/blob/master/internal/api/token_oidc.go)). CoursePro sends the raw nonce to Supabase and the SHA-256 hash to Apple, which matches. For a native-only app, Supabase says to put the bundle ID in "Client IDs" and that "native-only implementations don't require secret key rotation." It also says Supabase "does not support Server-to-Server notification endpoints, so you should leave that setting blank" ([Supabase docs](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/docs/content/guides/auth/social-login/auth-apple.mdx)). The on-device credential-state check CoursePro runs is the right substitute. Apple notes that when a user deletes their Apple Account, native apps get no `credentialRevokedNotification` and must use `getCredentialState(forUserID:)` ([TN3194](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple)).

**Private relay email.** Users who hide their email get a relay address. Apple says that to send them mail "you must register your outbound emails or email domains." All registered domains must be authenticated with SPF, DKIM or both, and Apple advises using both "whenever possible" ([Configuring your environment for Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/configuring-your-environment-for-sign-in-with-apple)). Each relay address can receive 100 emails a day ([Communicating using the private email relay service](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/communicating-using-the-private-email-relay-service)). App Review will not notice a missing registration. Real users will, though: welcome, email-change and receipt emails to them silently disappear.

**Two recommended polish items.** The HIG suggests showing "Using Sign in with Apple" in account settings, and suggests offering to link an Apple login to an existing email account when the emails match ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple)). Neither is a review requirement. Because the website does not offer Sign in with Apple, an account created with Apple on iPhone cannot sign in on the web yet. Adding it later means creating a Services ID and listing it "as the **first** entry in the Client IDs field," and the web OAuth secret then has to be rotated every six months ([Supabase docs](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/docs/content/guides/auth/social-login/auth-apple.mdx)).

|---|---|---|
| Entitlement `com.apple.developer.applesignin` | Yes (ios-native) | Enable the Sign in with Apple capability on App ID `app.canvaspro.mobile` in Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles; make sure Codemagic's provisioning profile includes it |
| System button, equal size, allowed title and colors | Yes | Check dark mode uses the white style; confirm no scrolling on small iPhones |
| No name/email re-prompt; save name once | Yes | None |

## Account deletion already meets 5.1.1(v); revocation depends on three server secrets

Guideline 5.1.1(v) says: "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple's support page defines deletion as removing "the account from the developer's records, along with any data associated with the account." It says "only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient." Non-regulated apps "should not require people to make a phone call, send an email, use a chat support flow." Confirmation steps are fine, but "apps that make it unnecessarily difficult … will not pass review." For Sign in with Apple, apps "should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens" ([Offering account deletion in your app](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/)). The standard rejection reads "The app supports account creation but does not include an option to initiate account deletion," and its suggested remedy is to reply "and identify where to locate this feature" ([Apple Developer Forums 765160](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765160)). That is why the review notes should state the path.

CoursePro's flow, More > Settings > Account, then type DELETE, satisfies every element. It is in-app, it deletes all data rather than deactivating, it explains what is removed and that removal is immediate, it cancels Paddle billing, and push tokens are removed by cascade. Typing DELETE is a normal confirmation, not "unnecessarily difficult." A short line saying the school's Canvas account itself is unaffected would help users understand what is happening. No Apple auto-renewable subscription exists, so Apple's "billing will continue through Apple" warning does not apply yet. It will apply if IAP is added.

Supabase does not revoke Apple tokens. A maintainer wrote that "Supabase Auth does not keep track of provider refresh and access tokens" and closed the request as not planned ([supabase/auth #1308](https://github.com/supabase/auth/issues/1308)). The native Swift flow also returns no Apple provider tokens ([supabase/auth #2155](https://github.com/supabase/auth/issues/2155)). CoursePro therefore takes the re-authenticate-at-deletion route. It gets a fresh authorization code and sends it to `src/lib/apple-sign-in.server.ts`. The server exchanges the code at `/auth/token` with an ES256 client secret and calls `/auth/revoke` before deleting. Apple documents `/auth/revoke` as "the only way to programmatically invalidate user tokens … without user interaction" ([TN3194](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple)). Apple's client-secret rules say `sub` must be the same App ID used as `client_id` (here `app.canvaspro.mobile`), and the expiry may be at most six months ([Creating a client secret](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accountorganizationaldatasharing/creating-a-client-secret)). Each primary app can have "a maximum of two private keys" ([Configuring your environment](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/configuring-your-environment-for-sign-in-with-apple)).

This is why the server secrets block submission even though nothing in the guidelines names them. A reviewer who signs in with Apple and then tests deletion runs exactly this path. If `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `APPLE_KEY_ID` or `APPLE_PRIVATE_KEY` is missing on the hosting, the Apple exchange fails in front of the reviewer. Apple's own fallback is also worth confirming in the code. TN3194 says that without a usable token "you must still fulfill the user's account deletion request" and should direct the user to revoke access manually ([TN3194](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple)). Deletion should therefore still complete, with a short message, if the user cancels the Apple re-confirmation or Apple's endpoint errors. Because `src/` runs on Cloudflare Workers, ES256 signing must use WebCrypto rather than Node `crypto`. Test the deployed route once with a real Apple account, and check that CoursePro then disappears from Settings > Apple Account > Sign in with Apple on the device. A 200 response alone does not prove revocation: one developer got a 200 because the token was invalid ([Apple Developer Forums 707181](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/707181)).

| Requirement | Already done in code | You must do |
|---|---|---|
| In-app deletion, full data, clear explanation | Yes (More > Settings > Account) | State the path in review notes |
| Apple token revocation | Yes (fresh code, server exchange plus revoke) | Create a Sign in with Apple key (.p8); set `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `APPLE_KEY_ID`, `APPLE_PRIVATE_KEY` on the website hosting; deploy main; test end to end |
| Fallback if revocation fails | Verify | Make sure deletion still completes and tells the user how to remove CoursePro under Apple Account settings |

## Turning on the paywall without in-app purchase would trigger 3.1.1 and 2.5.19

This is the largest *business* risk, and it lives in a database row, not the binary. Guideline 3.1.1 still reads: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app … you must use in-app purchase" ([App Review Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.1)). The multiplatform exception in 3.1.3(b) lets an app honor subscriptions bought on the web only "**provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app**." CoursePro is not a reader app and does not fit the closed list in 3.1.3(f) (VoIP, cloud storage, email, web hosting) ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Developer reports confirm how reviewers apply this. A B2B companion app with no purchase UI was rejected with "Your app accesses digital content purchased outside the app, and that content is not available through in-app purchase" ([Apple Developer Forums 811018](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811018)). A productivity app's "tier-aware" build was rejected, and a "**tier-identical**" build that gave every iOS user the same features passed ([Apple Developer Forums 825551](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825551)).

The October 16, 2026 revision tightened 2.5.19 to bar misrepresenting an app during review and adding features after approval, and it covers "bait-and-switch apps" ([Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)). Guideline 3.1.2(a) separately removes apps that "engage in bait-and-switch" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). CoursePro's iOS app shows no prices, subscription UI or Paddle links, and because `billing_config.enforce_from` is unset, everyone has access. Today that is a clean, tier-identical app. Once `enforce_from` is set, though, the Canvas data function returns `SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED` to unpaid iPhone users. A free app that approval saw fully working would then become locked behind a web-only purchase, which hits 3.1.1, 3.1.3(b) and 2.5.19 all at once.

The US carve-out does not rescue a no-IAP paywall. Since May 1, 2025, US-storefront apps may include "buttons, external links, or other calls to action" to web checkout with no entitlement ([Apple Developer News, May 1 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9txfddzf); [App Review Guidelines 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). That change lifted the anti-steering rules, not the IAP requirement itself. It is also under live litigation: the Ninth Circuit vacated the zero-commission ban in December 2025 ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/epic-v-apple-ninth-circuit-weighs)), and the Supreme Court granted certiorari on June 30, 2026 ([Oyez No. 25-1311](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2026/25-1311)). Outside the US, apps "and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action" toward non-IAP purchasing ([App Review Guidelines 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

The decision is binary. One option is to keep iOS fully free indefinitely, which means the backend must never return `SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED` to the iOS client, even after the web paywall goes live. The other is to ship StoreKit auto-renewable subscriptions in a reviewed build *before* enforcing. That build would put monthly and yearly plans in one subscription group, set the 14-day trial as an introductory offer, include a restore option, show the full renewal price as "the most prominent pricing element," and link the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy in both the app and the metadata ([Apple: Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). For this first submission, keep the app and its metadata free of "Pro" badges, "coming soon" teasers and pricing. Link in-app to specific policy and support pages, not to a homepage that shows prices; Apple has asked wrapper apps to remove links that "could indirectly provide access to external purchase mechanisms" ([Apple Developer Forums 684850](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/684850)).

| Requirement | Already done in code | You must do |
|---|---|---|
| No purchase UI, prices or web-checkout links | Yes | Keep metadata equally clean (no pricing, "Pro plan", "coming soon") |
| No paid gating without IAP | Yes today (`enforce_from` unset) | Decide: exempt iOS permanently, or add IAP in a reviewed build before enforcing. Never flip enforcement for iOS users without IAP |

## Privacy rules are mostly met; align the label and disclose the Canvas token

Guideline 5.1.1(i) requires "a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must state what is collected, how and why, confirm that third parties give equal protection, and "describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Guideline 1.5 requires a Support URL with "an easy way to contact you; this is particularly important for apps that may be used in the classroom" ([App Review Guidelines 1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.5)). Since the November 2025 update, 5.1.2(i) also requires developers to "clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, **including with third-party AI**, and obtain explicit permission" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). CoursePro's AI assistant screen now discloses what an approved assistant can read, and data flows only after the user approves on a consent screen. That satisfies the rule even under the stricter reading that reviewers expect an in-app consent step. The privacy policy should describe the same flow.

**Privacy manifest.** Apps that use required-reason APIs without declaring them "aren't accepted by App Store Connect" ([Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)). Reason **CA92.1** covers app-only defaults, and **1C8F.1** covers defaults shared within an App Group with extensions ([NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypeReasons](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/app-privacy-configuration/nsprivacyaccessedapitypes/nsprivacyaccessedapitypereasons)). Apple also requires that each bundle using such an API carry its own manifest ([Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)). CoursePro already has manifests for the app (CA92.1 and 1C8F.1) and for the widget (1C8F.1), with `NSPrivacyTracking` set to false. The remaining step is to archive and run Xcode's privacy report, to confirm that no other required-reason category is touched, for example `systemUptime` for timers (35F9.1) or file timestamps on cached data (C617.1). Supabase's Swift SDK is not on Apple's list of SDKs that need their own manifest ([Third-party SDK requirements](https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/)). Check any other Swift packages against that list.

**App Privacy label.** Apple defines "collect" as sending data off the device and keeping it longer than "necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." Data is "linked to the user's identity" unless it is de-identified before collection ([App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). The label must match the manifest's collected types: Name, Email Address, User ID, Device ID (push tokens), Other User Content (saved progress and study sessions) and Other Data Types (Canvas courses, assignments, grades and announcements, which Apple has no category for). Mark each as linked to the user, used for App Functionality, and not used for tracking. Apple says developers are "responsible for keeping your responses accurate" and can update the label without a new build ([App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)).

**The Canvas token on the server.** Background grade and due-date alerts are sent by server cron jobs that call Canvas, so the student's personal access token lives on CoursePro's backend. The 5.1.1(v) ban on storing tokens "off of the device" is written for "social networks" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Canvas is not one, so the text does not apply, but a reviewer could apply it by analogy. The safe posture is to follow its spirit. Before the user pastes a token, say that it is stored encrypted on CoursePro's servers to send alerts while the app is closed. Offer a disconnect control that deletes the token. Describe all of this in the privacy policy, which also covers guideline 1.6's call for "appropriate security measures" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). For export compliance, setting `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` to false is correct for an app that uses only OS HTTPS and CryptoKit hashing, since "encryption that's built into the operating system" is typically exempt ([Complying with encryption export regulations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)).

| Requirement | Already done in code | You must do |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy manifest (app plus widget) | Yes | Run Xcode's privacy report on the archive to confirm no undeclared categories |
| Tracking | `NSPrivacyTracking` false | Answer "No tracking" in App Privacy; no ATT prompt |
| AI sharing disclosure (5.1.2(i)) | Yes (disclosure plus consent screen) | Mirror it in the privacy policy |
| Privacy policy link in app and in ASC; Support URL | Verify in-app link | Publish working URLs with contact details |
| App Privacy label | n/a | Declare Name, Email, User ID, Device ID, Other User Content, Other Data Types: linked, App Functionality |
| Canvas token disclosure | Verify | Add pre-paste disclosure and a disconnect/delete-token control; encrypt at rest |

## Grade alerts and the notification prompt need small, low-cost changes

Guideline 4.5.4 says push notifications "must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The HIG adds: "Avoid including sensitive, personal, or confidential information in a notification … that could be visible to others" ([HIG: Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)). Apple has not said whether grades count as sensitive. Even so, "Good job! You scored 95% on Midterm" on a lock screen is the kind of text a reviewer can flag, and a lower score sent to a different student would be worse. A generic body by default, such as "New grade posted in BIO 101," with the score shown in-app and an opt-in "show scores in notifications" setting, removes the question. Setting `hiddenPreviewsBodyPlaceholder` helps too ([HIG: Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)). The Grades widget already hides while locked, which is consistent with this. Live Activities follow the same rule: the HIG says to "avoid displaying sensitive information" and to end them "immediately when the task or event ends" ([HIG: Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities)). The alert wording is built on the server push pipeline. The project's own rules require `npm test` and the live `/api/public/push/health` check after any change there, so make the wording change carefully rather than as a drive-by.

On timing, Apple advises making the request "in a context that helps people understand why your app needs authorization." Doing so "provides a better experience than automatically requesting authorization on first launch" ([Asking permission to use notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)). CoursePro's automatic request on the third app open is not a violation. It is delayed, but it is not contextual, and a reviewer who opens the app twice will never see it. Tell the reviewer in the notes how to enable notifications from the Settings toggle. Make sure every screen works with notifications denied, because 5.1.2(i) forbids requiring "system functionalities (such as push notifications …) in order to access functionality" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The only declared background mode is `remote-notification`. That is the safe choice: guideline 2.5.4 limits background modes to their intended purposes ([App Review Guidelines 2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.4)), so never add `audio` to keep the study timer alive.

## An 18+ account policy forces an 18+ age rating

On content alone, CoursePro has no violence, user-generated content, chat, ads or open web browser, so Apple's 2025 questionnaire would calculate **4+**. Apple's help text is unambiguous, though: "If your app has a EULA with minimum age requirements that exceed the rating that Apple calculated, you **must override** to a rating that adheres to the requirements." The override "will apply in all regions" ([Set an app age rating](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/set-an-app-age-rating)). Because new CoursePro accounts must confirm 18+, choose **Override to Higher Age Rating: 18+**, or lower the account minimum, which is a product and legal decision. Guideline 2.3.6 warns that a mis-rated app "could trigger an inquiry from government regulators" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The 18+ rating has costs. Press reports of Apple's February 2026 announcement say App Store downloads of 18+ apps in **Australia, Brazil and Singapore** are blocked unless the account is confirmed adult ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apple-expands-age-verification-api-global-compliance/)), and Screen Time restrictions will hide the app from minors. Answer "Unrestricted Web Access: no" only if Canvas links open in Safari or `SFSafariViewController` for specific URLs, because an embedded free-browsing view raises the rating to 16+ ([Age ratings values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)). Screenshots and icons must still suit a 4+ audience (2.3.8).

Age-assurance laws add a legal, not a review, obligation. Texas SB 2420 has applied to new Apple Accounts since **June 4, 2026** ([Apple Developer News, Jun 3 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne)). Press reports put Utah at May 6, 2026 and Louisiana at July 1, 2026 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/02/25/apple-expands-age-verification-controls/)). Apple's Q&A answers "If my app is rated 18+, do I still need to implement the Declared Age Range API? **Yes.** In regions where legally required." It also says "there are no changes to the App Review process," and that the full feature set needs Xcode 26.2 or later ([Age assurance frameworks Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance/)). The Declared Age Range API therefore does not block approval. It is a strong recommendation, and it is a more credible age gate than a checkbox. Only answer "Age Assurance" under In-App Controls if a real mechanism such as this API is implemented. A self-declared checkbox arguably does not "confirm" age.

## Name, metadata and Canvas IP: the rename fixed the easy rejection, not the policy question

Guideline 2.3.8 requires that the store name and on-device name "are similar to avoid creating confusion." A real rejection quoted the phrase "do not sufficiently match" ([App Review Guidelines 2.3.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.8); [velog.io developer post](https://velog.io/@chosh91/App-store-심사-리젝트-Guideline-2.3.8-Performance-Accurate-Metadata)). Guideline 4.1(c) bars using "another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name" ([App Review Guidelines 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#copycats)). CoursePro's `CFBundleDisplayName` and widget names already read "CoursePro," which closes both issues. Keep the bundle ID `app.canvaspro.mobile`: it is not user-facing, and Sign in with Apple identities are tied to it. Make sure the App Store Connect seller or developer name is not "CanvasPro," because 5.2.1 covers misleading names "in your app bundle or developer name" ([App Review Guidelines 5.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.1)). Under 2.3.7, keep "Canvas," "Instructure" and competitor names out of the subtitle and keywords. A factual "Works with Canvas LMS" line in the description, paired with the non-affiliation disclaimer the app already shows, is ordinary descriptive use. Under 4.4, name the widgets and Live Activities in the description ([App Review Guidelines 4.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#extensions)). Under 4.3(b), which now lists "simple timers" as a saturated category ([App Review Guidelines 4.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#spam)), present the Pomodoro feature as part of an assignment-aware study workflow, not the headline. Screenshots must "show the app in use, and not merely the title art, login page" ([2.3.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.3)) and must use "fictional account information" ([2.3.9](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.9)). The review account's sample data is ideal for that. An iPhone-only app needs the 6.9-inch iPhone set, and no iPad set unless it supports iPadOS ([Screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications)). It will still run on iPad in compatibility mode, so test it there.

The harder issue is guideline 5.2.2. An app that "uses, accesses … or displays content from a third-party service" must be "specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request" ([App Review Guidelines 5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.2)). Canvas's own API documentation says that "asking any other user to manually generate a token and enter it into your application is a violation of Canvas' API Policy." It also says "Applications in use by multiple users MUST use OAuth" ([Canvas API docs: OAuth2, UBC mirror](https://canvas.ubc.ca/doc/api/file.oauth.html)). That quote comes from search excerpts of a mirror, because Instructure's own pages could not be fetched, so re-verify it. Some universities also disable student tokens ([University of Missouri System KB](https://tdx.umsystem.edu/TDClient/66/MOOnline/KB/ArticleDet?ID=2193)). No public 5.2.2 rejection of a Canvas companion app turned up, and reviewers rarely probe third-party terms unless a rights holder complains or the branding invites scrutiny. The realistic exposure is therefore a post-launch Instructure complaint, more than a first-review rejection.

Don't let this block submission. Do prepare. Have a short statement ready explaining that the student authorizes access to their own data, that the token is encrypted on the server for background alerts, and that it can be revoked in Canvas or deleted in CoursePro. Avoid making token-pasting the star of the metadata. Over time, add Canvas OAuth through `ASWebAuthenticationSession` for schools that issue a developer key, or seek written permission from Instructure. Apple accepts "email correspondence showing you have permission" as rights documentation ([Complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)).

**Build requirements.** Since April 28, 2026, uploads must be built "with Xcode 26 or later using an SDK for iOS 26." Since September 9, 2026, the minimum deployment target is iOS 13 ([Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)). CoursePro's iOS 17 target is fine, but the Codemagic image must use Xcode 26, and 26.2 or later if the Declared Age Range API is added. Building with the iOS 26 SDK also adopts the new system look, so check custom tab bars, the sidebar indicator and widgets on a real iOS 26 device in light and dark mode. A broken layout is a 2.1 or 4.0 risk.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all of these rules is that CoursePro's remaining risk sits outside the Swift code. Sign in with Apple and account deletion are implemented to Apple's documented standard, but they depend on settings in three other systems: the Apple Developer portal, Supabase, and the website's secrets. A reviewer exercises all three within minutes of opening the app. The paywall is a policy decision expressed as a single database value, and the October 2026 tightening of 2.5.19 means it can no longer be treated as a server detail to flip after approval. For iPhone users it has to be settled now, in favor of permanent free access or of IAP shipped in a reviewed build.

The two issues likeliest to matter after approval, Canvas's prohibition on pasted tokens and lock-screen grade text, are unlikely to fail the first review. Both are the kind of thing that turns into a removal notice or a school complaint later. The cheapest insurance is to fix the grade-alert wording now and start the Canvas OAuth or Instructure-permission track in parallel with launch. One App Review appointment, a 30-minute Webex where developers can ask "how your app can best align with guidelines" ([Apple: App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)), would also be a good use of time before submission. It could pre-clear both the sample-data review account and the payment plan in a single conversation.
